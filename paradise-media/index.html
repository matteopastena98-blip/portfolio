How Twitch Streamers Actually Make Money: Why Follower Count Lies (and Where the Cash Comes From)

A channel with 60,000 followers can struggle to clear Twitch's $50 payout floor, while a 90-viewer niche regular quietly pays their rent. Here is the real math behind subscriptions, ad density, Bits, and off-platform deals.

By Matteo Pastena 5 min read + interactive tools Verified against official Twitch Creator rules

If you want to guess how much a Twitch streamer earns, ignore their follower count. Followers are a historical souvenir: they show how many people clicked a heart button during a viral clip three years ago, not how many show up on a Tuesday night.

Everything in live-streaming economics runs on a single number instead: average concurrent viewers (CCV)—how many people are watching at any given minute. Concurrent viewers buy subscriptions, sit through mid-roll ad breaks, cheer Bits during tense moments, and set the price floor when a sponsor asks for a deliverable.

"Followers measure who liked a clip three years ago. Concurrent viewers measure who is in the room when the rent is due."

1. Where a $5.99 Subscription Actually Goes

Subscriptions are the backbone of most mid-sized channels because they recur monthly. In the United States, a standard Tier 1 subscription costs $5.99 per month, though Twitch adjusts prices locally across countries so viewers outside North America and Western Europe can subscribe at lower rates.

Crucially, Twitch does not split the gross sticker price. First, local sales taxes (like VAT), currency conversion, and payment processing fees come off the top. Only then is the net revenue divided—at 50/50 by default, or up to 60/40 and 70/30 if the creator qualifies for the Twitch Plus Program.

4. The Four Real Milestones From Zero to Full-Time

Twitch lowered its onboarding barriers so small creators can test monetization early, but unlocking the buttons and earning a living remain miles apart:

  1. Stage 1 · Day One
    Monetization for All (Tools Unlocked, No Cashout Yet): Most streamers can enable subscriptions and Bits early, or spend small US balances back on Twitch—but to withdraw real cash to a bank or PayPal account, you still need Affiliate status and at least $50 USD accrued ($100 for wire transfers) on Twitch's Net-15 payout cycle.
  2. Stage 2 · 3+ CCV
    Twitch Affiliate: Unlocked after hitting 25 followers, streaming 4 hours across 4 different days, and averaging 3 concurrent viewers in a 30-day window. Unlocks cash payouts, custom emotes, channel points, and ad revenue share.
  3. Stage 3 · 100 Pts
    Plus Program Level 1 (60/40 Split): Open to both Affiliates and Partners who maintain 100 Plus Points (e.g., 100 paid Tier 1 subs, or fewer Tier 2/Tier 3 subs) for three consecutive months, locking in a 60% net cut for 12 months.
  4. Stage 4 · 75+ CCV
    Twitch Partner & Plus Level 2 (70/30 Split): Partner requires manual review—typically around 75+ average concurrent viewers excluding raids and embeds. Hitting 300 Plus Points for three straight months unlocks the top 70/30 net split with no annual earnings cap.

The Bottom Line for Aspiring Creators (and Viewers)

Twitch is a lousy discovery engine—its homepage rewards channels that already have a crowd—but it remains one of the strongest retention and monetization engines online once a viewer cares enough to stay for two hours. The creators who last a decade treat Twitch as the live living room at the center of their business: YouTube and short-form clips bring strangers through the door, concurrent viewership pays the baseline bills via 60/40 or 70/30 subs and the 55% ad split, and sponsorships or owned products turn a dedicated chat of 150 regulars into a real career.
